In April 2026, Gemini Lab's operator Masaki Hirokawa ([profile](https://dolice.design)) visited government offices in Toyama Prefecture, Japan, to negotiate the resolution of a wrongful taxation issue spanning over five years.

## Background for International Readers

I am an independent app developer whose primary revenue comes from AdMob, Google's mobile advertising platform. Under Japan's Consumption Tax Act (Articles 7 and 17), revenue from providing services to overseas entities — which includes digital advertising platforms operated by Google (a US-based company) — is exempt from consumption tax.

Despite this clear legal basis, I was charged approximately ¥5 million ($33,000 USD) in consumption tax over several years. My 2021 refund request was denied by the Miyazaki Tax Office. By 2024, the statute of limitations had conveniently expired, and the Uozu Tax Office demanded I repay the tax-exempt treatment I'd received in the interim. My current monthly income is approximately ¥45,000 ($300 USD), while I am asked to repay ¥60,668 ($400 USD) per month — a mathematical impossibility.

## Local Offices Respond Constructively

Both the Nyuzen Town Office and the Namerikawa City Tax Division responded that my case is "under review" — a positive sign indicating formal internal deliberation rather than dismissal.

## Uozu Tax Office — Refusal and a Troubling Reaction to Recording

The Uozu Tax Office told an entirely different story. The assigned officer, referred to as Mr. Y, refused to meet face to face. Through a counter-window exchange, I explained the legal basis for my claim in detail. Mr. Y offered no substantive response, merely repeating the office's existing position.

When I informed him that I was recording the conversation, he immediately ended it: "I cannot continue this discussion any further."

A public servant performing official duties should have nothing to fear from documentation. This reaction speaks volumes about the office's awareness of its own indefensible position. At this point, Uozu Tax Office is effectively complicit in perpetuating wrongful taxation.

## The Knowledge Gap That Causes Wrongful Taxation

At the heart of this issue lies a structural flaw shared across Japan's tax administration and its professional tax advisory industry: **a widespread lack of expertise regarding the taxation of digital export services.**

Japanese tax law is unambiguous — digital advertising revenue from platforms like AdMob constitutes a service provided to an overseas entity, exempt from consumption tax. Yet in practice, most Japanese tax accountants classify this revenue as ordinary domestic income subject to consumption tax. Tax office personnel make the same error. Physical goods exports are easily recognized as tax-exempt, but digital service exports remain a blind spot.

This is not an isolated mistake — it is a systemic failure that generates wrongful taxation cases affecting countless freelancers and independent developers throughout Japan.
