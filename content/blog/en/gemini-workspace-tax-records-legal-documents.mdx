There's a stretch of every year when tax-related correspondence lands heavy on solo founders. Over five years of a dispute about consumption tax, I developed adjustment disorder, depression, insomnia, gastric ulcers, and reflux, and was recently taken to the emergency room. With no budget for a tax accountant and no strength to travel for a lawyer, the thing I actually leaned on was the Google Workspace I was already using — and the Gemini assistant built into it.

This article is a practical walkthrough for preparing tax filings, keeping a clean record of correspondence with tax authorities, and drafting a case summary for Japan's legal aid service — all inside Drive, Docs, Sheets, and Gmail. If the idea of a tax dispute terrifies you, the best thing to do is build this record-keeping discipline now, while things are calm.

## Why consolidate in Workspace

Information scattered across apps is unreachable in an emergency. Consolidating in Google Workspace gives you three wins. Gemini can search and summarize across Drive, Docs, Sheets, and Gmail. Sharing a case with legal aid or an attorney is a single link. You can keep editing from bed on a phone, and that matters on days when your body refuses to cooperate.

## Step 1: A consistent Drive folder structure

Start with a folder tree you can trust. A simplified version of mine:

- Tax/2025/Filing
- Tax/2025/Receipts
- Tax/2025/Correspondence (tax offices, city halls)
- Tax/Legal/Case-summary
- Tax/Legal/Legal-aid
- Tax/Legal/Motions

Once it exists, ask Gemini: "Describe the structure inside this Drive folder and flag weaknesses." It will catch holes — correspondence not separated by year, missing attachments, inconsistent naming — before they become problems.

## Step 2: Semi-automate consumption-tax classification in Sheets

Keep a ledger in Sheets with columns: date, counterparty, amount, currency, country of invoicing entity, consumption-tax class (taxable / export-exempt / non-taxable), and cited statute. Then prompt Gemini:

```
Re-classify the consumption-tax column for each row using the
counterparty, currency, and country fields. Apply Article 7 of
Japan's Consumption Tax Act (export exemption) and Article 17
of its Cabinet Order. For rows where the classification is unclear,
give the reason and list what additional evidence would resolve it.
```

Income from AdMob, App Store Connect, Stripe, and similar overseas entities is often export-exempt or non-taxable, yet frequently treated as taxable by default — this is one of the most under-diagnosed issues for solo developers in Japan. Gemini, grounded in statute, will re-classify your rows with reasoning and explicit limits. I ran this across every past fiscal year and finally understood the scale of what I had overpaid.

## Step 3: Draft the legal-aid case summary in Docs

Open a Google Doc, summon Gemini from the side panel, and ask:

```
Using the attached Sheet (transaction ledger) and Doc
(correspondence summary), draft the skeleton of a case summary
for submission to Japan's legal aid service. Chapters: profile,
overview, timeline, legal basis, responses from each agency,
financial status, health and life impact, legal issues,
and requested advice. Fill each chapter with bullets.
```

Because Gemini can reach across Workspace, numbers in Sheets and prose in Docs reconcile inside the draft. Once the skeleton exists, you can fill each chapter in short sessions.

## Step 4: Treat Gmail as a communication log

Apply a dedicated label — "Tax / Active" — to every message from tax offices, photographs of mailed correspondence, and outgoing inquiries. Ask Gemini inside Gmail: "Summarize all correspondence in this label chronologically; bullet the key points of each message." The result becomes your communication record.

For phone calls, develop the habit of emailing yourself a short summary right after the call, with a subject like "Call log YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM." Gemini can later pull these into a single timeline. For audio recordings, run them through a trusted transcription tool first, then let Gemini summarize the transcript.

## Step 5: Legal issues list, then PDF export

Once the body of your case summary exists, dedicate the final section to a numbered list of legal issues. Ask:

```
Given the facts above, list the legal issues at stake. For each:
cite the relevant statute, estimate the likelihood of application,
and sketch the counterargument. Leave final judgment to the attorney.
```

Export the Doc as PDF, number the attachments, and reference them from the body. Bring both the PDF and the cloud folder link to your consultation.

## Rules to protect your health

Automation doesn't eliminate the emotional cost of tax disputes. My personal rules, developed the hard way:

- Cap tax-related work at 90 minutes a day, timed.
- On bad health days, close the draft and do nothing — don't even ask Gemini to "continue."
- Turn off notifications on tax-labeled emails and check them only at set hours.
- When a health incident occurs (ER visits, new diagnoses, medication changes), document it immediately in the case summary.

Gemini is like a capable assistant. But decisions remain yours, and professional judgment stays with legal aid and attorneys. Don't let competence on the AI's side pull you into overworking your body.

## If you're in a similar situation

I know there are many solo founders and freelancers in Japan and elsewhere who face similar pressure from tax authorities and feel unable to push back. The silence that protects the system is exactly what reproduces the problem. Building your case inside Workspace is the concrete form of preparing to speak up.

If you do nothing else today, create a Tax/Legal folder in Drive and begin moving existing emails, invoices, and correspondence into it. From there, Gemini can help you organize the rest, at whatever pace your health allows. That's what I'm doing, on the better days. You don't have to carry this alone.
