"NotebookLM Pro versus Advanced versus Ultra — which one do I actually need to get the latest Gemini?" That question keeps landing in my inbox, and after the third time this week I decided it was worth a dedicated post. Google retired the *Gemini Advanced* brand back in 2024, but the term still floats around search results, blog posts, and YouTube thumbnails as if it were a current SKU. Layer NotebookLM's own historical pricing on top, and the naming becomes genuinely confusing.

This post is a 2026-current map of **which Gemini model NotebookLM uses inside each subscription tier**, what limits you actually hit, and how to decide whether the upgrade is worth your money.

## First, let's untangle the names

The "Pro vs Advanced vs Ultra" three-way comparison people search for doesn't really exist anymore. Here is the present-day reality:

- **Gemini Advanced** — A premium plan that launched in early 2024. It was rebranded to **Google AI Pro** during 2025, and the *Advanced* name is no longer in use as of 2026
- **Google AI Pro** — The successor to Gemini Advanced. A monthly subscription that bundles the higher NotebookLM quota (formerly sold as NotebookLM Plus)
- **Google AI Ultra** — A new top-tier plan introduced in 2025. It adds the Deep Think reasoning mode, research previews like Project Mariner, and substantially expanded generation quotas
- **NotebookLM Plus** — Briefly sold standalone in 2024, now folded into Google AI Pro and Ultra rather than a separate product

So if you were searching for "Advanced," you almost certainly mean **Google AI Pro** today. The rest of this article uses the current naming throughout.

## Which Gemini model each tier actually runs

NotebookLM doesn't expose a model picker in its UI, but the underlying model that handles your questions varies clearly by plan.

- **Free tier** — Powered primarily by the Gemini 2.5 Flash family. Responses are quick, and basic summarization and Q&A work well. You will feel a ceiling once you push into long-form synthesis or multi-source reasoning
- **Google AI Pro** — NotebookLM begins routing your queries to **Gemini 2.5 Pro–class** reasoning. Long-document summarization sharpens noticeably, and cross-source consistency improves. This is the tier where "I trust the answer" starts becoming the norm rather than the exception
- **Google AI Ultra** — Includes everything in AI Pro, plus access to **Deep Think** (a slower but more thorough reasoning mode) and significantly higher daily generation budgets. Inside NotebookLM, demanding analytical questions trade some latency for noticeably more comprehensive coverage

A subtle but important point: NotebookLM is a hybrid pipeline, not a single model. It picks among the Gemini family based on the nature of each request, so "I'm always getting the top model" is not quite accurate. The right mental model is that **your subscription decides which ceiling you can reach**, not that every keystroke triggers the most expensive inference.

## Source limits and generation quotas

Model quality matters, but for most users the deciding factor is the raw quotas. Here is the lay of the land as of April 2026, drawn from the official help center.

- **Free tier**:
  - Notebooks: 100
  - Sources per notebook: 50
  - Daily questions: 50
  - Audio Overviews: 3 per day
- **Google AI Pro**:
  - Notebooks: 500
  - Sources per notebook: 300
  - Daily questions: 500
  - Audio Overviews: 20 per day, with custom instructions and interactive audio unlocked
  - Video Overviews: 5 per day
- **Google AI Ultra**:
  - All AI Pro limits, plus priority queueing
  - Video Overviews: 20 per day
  - Deep Think reasoning quota

Numbers ramp up evenly across tiers, but **the upgrade you feel most viscerally is the jump from 50 to 300 sources per notebook**. If you do any kind of serious research, the 50-source wall arrives shockingly fast. On the other hand, if your workflow is "read a textbook or two and ask follow-up questions," the free tier remains genuinely capable.

## How to decide which tier you actually need

Here is the decision framework I personally use, based on workload rather than feature lists.

### Stay on the free tier if

- You read a few books or papers per month and want NotebookLM as a study aid
- You're a student pulling quotes from one or two textbooks for assignments
- You're a writer organizing the dozen-or-so source PDFs you already have

At that intensity, 50 sources and three audio overviews a day are not the constraint. Paying for capacity you don't use is the more expensive mistake.

### Upgrade to Google AI Pro when

- **Document analysis is part of your daily job** — Meeting notes, specs, and reports stack up to 300 sources within two or three weeks if you treat NotebookLM as your second brain
- **You actually use Audio Overviews for sharing or commute listening** — Twenty per day plus custom instructions makes them a real workflow tool, not a curiosity
- **You're amortizing the subscription across the whole Gemini ecosystem** — AI Pro is not a NotebookLM-only fee. Compare its value across Gmail, Docs, and the Gemini app together, not in isolation

I've put together a detailed cost and feature comparison in [Google AI Pro vs Ultra — Pricing and Services Compared](/en/articles/gemini-basics/google-ai-pro-ultra-pricing-services-complete-guide-2026) if you want to dig into the specifics.

### Consider Google AI Ultra only if

- You work in research, consulting, or editorial roles where **slow, deeper reasoning has tangible payoff**
- You actually want to evaluate research-preview features like Project Mariner or Veo 3 in your daily flow
- You routinely hit the AI Pro ceilings on Audio and Video Overviews

Ultra's price jump is real. My recommendation is to **run AI Pro for one or two months first, and only move up once you find yourself routinely capped**. Going straight to Ultra often results in unused capacity and buyer's remorse. ## When the API is the better answer

NotebookLM as a product can't be replicated through the API alone, but if all you really want is access to **Gemini 2.5 Pro / 3.1-class quality**, the API is often the cleaner path.

```python
# Install: pip install google-genai
# Set GEMINI_API_KEY in your environment
import os
from google import genai

client = genai.Client(api_key=os.environ["GEMINI_API_KEY"])

# Use a stable alias for development
# - "gemini-2.5-pro-latest" tracks the current stable release
# - In production, pin to a specific versioned string for reproducibility
response = client.models.generate_content(
    model="gemini-2.5-pro-latest",
    contents="List the five most important points from this PDF, citing the source paragraph for each.",
)

print(response.text)
# Expected output: five bullet points, each ending with "(paragraph N)"
```

With direct API access you bypass NotebookLM's source caps entirely and can build your own RAG pipeline. Pay-as-you-go pricing also wins for many small workloads — for a solo developer running "a few dozen long summaries per month," the API often costs less than a flat AI Pro subscription. Production patterns are documented in [Gemini 2.5 Pro Latest API Production Guide](/en/articles/gemini-api/gemini-25-pro-latest-api-production-guide).

## What NotebookLM still does that the API can't

People sometimes ask, "If the API exists, why bother with NotebookLM?" My answer is that **NotebookLM's real value is the UI and citation fidelity**, not raw model access. Three things stand out:

- **Automatic citations** — Every claim in a response links back to its source paragraph. Building this from scratch eats an entire day of PDF parsing, chunk management, and citation mapping
- **One-click Audio and Video Overviews** — Behind the scenes a multi-model pipeline produces these, and you get to call it from a button. That ratio of effort to output is hard to beat
- **Built-in collaboration** — Notebook-level sharing is a first-class concept, which makes it the path of least resistance for research labs, editorial teams, and study groups

Conversely, if you're using it solo, want to embed it in Slack or your own product, or have data that genuinely cannot leave your premises, the API is the better scaffold.
