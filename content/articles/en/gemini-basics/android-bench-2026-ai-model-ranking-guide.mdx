Phone benchmarks used to mean AnTuTu scores and GeekBench numbers. In 2026, there's a new dimension that matters just as much for AI-enabled devices: how fast and efficiently can this phone run an AI model locally?

Android Bench is Google's answer to that question — an open benchmarking suite that measures real AI inference performance on Android hardware. With the launch of Gemma 4 and its integration into Android AICore, Android Bench has become a meaningful tool for understanding what "on-device AI" actually delivers on current hardware.

## What Android Bench Measures

Android Bench runs standardized AI workloads on actual Android devices and measures three main things:

**① Token generation speed (tokens per second)**: How fast a text generation model produces output. This maps directly to how natural a conversational AI feels — too slow, and it becomes painful to use.

**② Image classification and object detection throughput**: Relevant for camera AI, augmented reality, and visual search features.

**③ Energy efficiency (tokens per joule)**: How much battery a given inference task consumes. On-device AI's main promise — offline and private processing — only holds up if it doesn't drain your battery in minutes.

## Gemma 4 and Android Bench

Gemma 4, released by Google in April 2026, is a primary test subject for Android Bench. The 27B MoE (Mixture of Experts) variant is specifically optimized for mobile hardware, and Android Bench exists partly to quantify how well different Android devices can run it.

With Gemma 4 integrated into Android AICore's Developer Preview (initially on Pixel devices), there's now a practical reason to care about these numbers: they predict how well local AI features will work on a given device.

### Reference: Approximate Gemma 4 Inference Speeds by Device Class

These are approximate figures for early 2026 hardware:

- **Snapdragon 8 Elite devices**: ~20–30 tokens/second (comfortable for conversational use)
- **MediaTek Dimensity 9400 devices**: ~18–25 tokens/second
- **Mid-range (Snapdragon 7s generation)**: ~5–10 tokens/second (usable but noticeably slower)

The practical threshold: roughly 20 tokens/second is where text generation starts feeling responsive rather than choppy in a conversational UI. Below 10 tokens/second, users notice the wait.

## How to Read the Numbers

A few things to keep in mind when comparing Android Bench results:

### Model size and quantization matter enormously

The same device will produce very different numbers depending on which model size you're running and at what quantization level (INT4 vs INT8 vs FP16). When comparing results across sources, verify you're looking at the same model configuration — Gemma 4's 4B and 27B variants are not directly comparable.

### Efficiency matters as much as raw speed

A device that generates 30 tokens/second but drains 15% battery per minute isn't practically better than one doing 20 tokens/second at 8% per minute. Always check the tokens-per-joule figure alongside raw throughput.

### Benchmark conditions are best-case

Android Bench runs under near-ideal conditions: minimal background processes, controlled thermal state. Real-world performance while multitasking can be meaningfully lower — typically 70–80% of benchmark figures is a reasonable expectation.

## Why On-Device AI Matters Now

The central advantage of on-device AI over cloud AI is independence from a network connection and a server. That matters in several ways:

- **Privacy**: Your data never leaves the device
- **Offline use**: Flights, subways, remote areas — the AI still works
- **Latency**: No round-trip to a server for short, local tasks
- **Cost**: No API metering or usage fees for the end user

The historical limitation was quality: locally-runnable models couldn't match cloud models in capability. Gemma 4 narrows that gap significantly for everyday tasks — text summarization, translation, code completion at the IDE level — even if it doesn't match the frontier capability of Gemini 2.5 Pro or GPT-5.

## Gemini Nano: The Connection to the Gemini Family

Android Bench evaluates more than just Gemma models. Google's Gemini Nano — the on-device variant of the Gemini model family — also runs through similar evaluations.

Gemini Nano is already deployed on Pixel devices for features like Smart Reply, document summarization, and real-time translation. Android Bench metrics for Gemini Nano give a measurable picture of how well these native device features will perform on a given piece of hardware.

For app developers, this creates an actionable framework: Android Bench numbers tell you what AI features you can realistically ship on a given device class. If your target audience has mid-range hardware, benchmark data helps you scope what's feasible locally versus what should remain in the cloud.

## Where to Start

Android Bench is available as a developer preview on Google Play for Android 14+ devices. If you want to see how your own device performs, you can run the benchmark directly.

For hands-on Gemma 4 experimentation, the [Google AI Edge SDK documentation](https://ai.google.dev/edge) is the clearest starting point. Running a local inference for the first time — even on a mid-range phone — is a useful reminder of how much the on-device AI story has changed in the last two years.
