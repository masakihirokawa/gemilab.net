Gemini Gems empowers you to create personalized AI assistants tailored to your exact needs. Yet many users encounter a frustrating problem: custom instructions don't seem to work as expected.

If you've ever set up detailed instructions only to find Gemini ignoring them, you're not alone. This guide walks you through the most common reasons why custom instructions fail and how to fix them.

## What Are Gemini Gems Custom Instructions?

Gemini Gems is a powerful customization feature within Google Gemini that lets you create AI assistants with a specific personality, expertise level, and communication style. Custom instructions act as persistent guidelines that shape how Gemini responds within that Gem—across all conversations.

**Where to find them:**

- Google AI Studio at [ai.google.dev](https://ai.google.dev) under the "Gems" section
- Gemini.google.com via the settings menu for individual Gems

**Basic setup:**

1. Log into Gemini.google.com or Google AI Studio
2. Create a new Gem or edit an existing one
3. Enter your custom instructions in the "Instructions" field
4. Save

It sounds simple, but the devil is in the details. Most users discover that writing effective instructions requires precision and clarity.

## Common Reasons Why Custom Instructions Fail

If your instructions aren't working as intended, one of these issues likely explains why.

### Vague or Contradictory Instructions

The most common culprit is instructions that lack specificity. Saying "be more professional" or "explain in detail" leaves too much room for interpretation.

**Weak instruction**: "You're a marketing expert. Help with my campaign."

**Strong instruction**: "You are a B2B SaaS marketing manager with 8 years of experience. When I ask about campaigns, provide recommendations in JSON format with fields: target_segment, key_message, and recommended_channels. Always prioritize data-driven approaches over trends."

Contradictory instructions also confuse Gemini. Asking it to be both "brief" and "comprehensive" creates conflicting priorities that lead to unpredictable outputs.

### Version and Plan Limitations

Gemini has multiple versions (gemini-1.5-flash, gemini-2.0-flash, gemini-1.5-pro) with varying capabilities. The free tier and Gemini Advanced plan support different features. Some advanced customization options only work with specific model versions or paid plans.

Custom instructions you set in the free version may behave differently when used with Gemini Advanced or via API.

### Browser Cache and Session Issues

Your browser might be serving cached versions of Gemini that predate your instruction updates. If you've been using the same conversation for hours, older context can override newer instructions.

Gemini sometimes prioritizes the active conversation history over system-level custom instructions, especially after an extended chat session.

### Character Limit Exceeded

Gemini Gems has a character limit for custom instructions (typically a few thousand characters). Instructions longer than this may be truncated without warning. Always verify that your full instruction text was saved by reopening the edit screen.

### Long Conversation Context Overrides Instructions

As conversations grow longer, recent user messages start to take precedence over system-level custom instructions. If you casually say "talk more casually" midway through a chat, that instruction might override your original custom directive to "maintain professional tone."

Gemini weights recent, direct user input more heavily than system-level instructions.

## How to Write Custom Instructions That Actually Work

The real solution is understanding instruction design. Here's what separates effective instructions from weak ones.

### The Three-Part Structure: Role → Format → Constraints

Strong instructions follow this pattern:

1. **Role Definition**: Clearly state expertise and context
2. **Output Format**: Specify exactly how responses should be structured
3. **Constraints**: List any limitations or requirements

### Real-World Examples

#### Example 1: Full-Stack Engineer Assistant

```
You are an experienced full-stack web engineer with 10 years in production environments. 
When answering coding questions, follow these rules:

Output Format:
- Include working code examples with language markers (```python, ```javascript, etc.)
- Highlight performance and security implications
- Explain the "why" before the "how"

Constraints:
- Responses should be 500-800 words
- Prioritize modern best practices over legacy approaches
- Always flag security vulnerabilities explicitly
- Suggest testing strategies when relevant
```

#### Example 2: Content Editor Assistant

```
You are a professional editor specializing in marketing and product documentation.
When editing user-provided content, apply these standards:

Output Format:
- Present suggestions in before/after comparison format
- Include a one-sentence reason for each change
- End with a brief paragraph on overall tone and style

Constraints:
- Limit suggestions to 5-10 changes maximum (avoid overwhelming the writer)
- Preserve the author's original intent and voice
- Use consistent formality (formal, casual, etc.)
- Flag unclear passages rather than rewriting them
```

#### Example 3: Data Analyst Assistant

```
You are a data analyst experienced with business intelligence and metrics design.
When discussing metrics, dashboards, or KPIs, follow this structure:

Output Format:
- Define each metric with its calculation method
- Explain business relevance in 1-2 sentences
- Flag common pitfalls or misinterpretations
- Recommend tracking frequency (daily, weekly, etc.)

Constraints:
- Assume the user is non-technical but business-smart
- Suggest specific tools when applicable (Google Analytics, Amplitude, etc.)
- Relate recommendations back to actual business outcomes
```

## Step-by-Step Troubleshooting When Instructions Don't Apply

If you've set instructions but they're not taking effect, work through these steps methodically.

### Step 1: Refresh the Application

The simplest fix often works:

1. Close all Gemini tabs completely
2. Clear your browser cache (or use Ctrl+Shift+Delete / Cmd+Shift+Delete)
3. Open a new tab and navigate to Gemini.google.com
4. Open your Gem

A fresh page load ensures you're not viewing cached content.

### Step 2: Start a New Conversation

Old conversations carry accumulated context that might override your custom instructions.

1. After updating instructions, don't continue the old conversation
2. Click "New Conversation" or equivalent in your Gem's UI
3. Ask the same question in the fresh conversation
4. Compare responses

New conversations apply custom instructions from scratch.

### Step 3: Delete and Recreate the Gem

If refreshing doesn't work, start over:

1. Delete the problematic Gem entirely
2. Create a new Gem with the same name
3. Carefully re-enter your instructions (double-check for clarity and length)
4. Save and test immediately

This eliminates any lingering configuration conflicts.

### Step 4: Test in Incognito/Private Mode

Browser extensions or saved preferences might interfere:

1. Open an incognito or private window
2. Log into Gemini.google.com
3. Test the same Gem with a sample query
4. Compare behavior to your regular browser

If the Gem works correctly in private mode, clear your browser cache and cookies in normal mode.

### Step 5: Sign Out Completely and Reset Session

Sometimes your Google session itself is stale:

1. Log out of Gemini.google.com
2. Close your browser entirely
3. Wait 2-3 minutes or restart your computer
4. Log back in
5. Test your Gem

This forces Google's servers to reload your profile and custom settings.

## Getting Maximum Value from Gemini Gems

Once your custom instructions work reliably, here's how to build a powerful personal AI assistant toolkit.

### Create Task-Specific Gems

Rather than one "general-purpose" Gem, build several specialized ones:

- "Product Strategist" Gem for roadmap planning
- "Code Reviewer" Gem for technical feedback
- "Writing Editor" Gem for content polish
- "Brainstorm Partner" Gem for ideation

Each Gem can have hyper-specific instructions optimized for its role.

### Continuously Refine Instructions Based on Real Usage

Your first version won't be perfect. After a week of using a Gem, revisit the instructions:

- Does it misunderstand recurring questions? → Clarify that area
- Are outputs in the wrong format? → Update the format specification
- Do you wish it asked follow-up questions? → Add that to constraints

The best Gems are living documents, not static configurations.

### Build a Workflow Across Multiple Gems

Chain Gems together in your creative or analytical process:

1. "Brainstormer" Gem generates raw ideas
2. "Critic" Gem pokes holes in those ideas
3. "Executor" Gem turns viable ideas into action steps
4. "Editor" Gem polishes the final output

This structured approach produces better results than asking one Gem to do everything.
