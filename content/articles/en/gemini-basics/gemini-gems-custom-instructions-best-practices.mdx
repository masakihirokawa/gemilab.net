## What Are Gemini Gems?

Google Gemini's **Gems** feature lets you build custom AI assistants tailored to specific tasks. Instead of re-explaining your context every time you open a new chat, you define your Gem's role, personality, and rules upfront — and Gemini follows them consistently every time you use it.

As of 2026, Gems are available with Gemini Advanced (Google One AI Premium plan). Think of a Gem as a saved "configuration" for Gemini: a specialist you've trained to work the way you need.

The most important part of creating a useful Gem is writing clear, structured **custom instructions**. This guide shows you exactly how.

---

## Why Custom Instructions Matter

Custom instructions are the "brain" of your Gem. They tell Gemini who it is, what it's for, how to behave, and what to avoid. The more specific and well-organized your instructions, the closer Gemini's responses will be to what you actually want.

Without clear instructions, Gemini defaults to generic assistant mode — helpful, but not specialized. With well-written instructions, your Gem becomes a reliable expert that works exactly the way you designed it.

**Custom instructions matter most when you need:**

- A consistent tone or writing style (e.g., always formal, always concise)
- Domain-specific expertise (e.g., respond only from a legal perspective)
- A structured workflow (e.g., always ask clarifying questions before suggesting solutions)
- Enforced guardrails (e.g., never recommend products without a cited source)

## The Four-Part Structure of Effective Custom Instructions

The most reliable approach to writing custom instructions uses four components:

### 1. Persona (Who Is This Gem?)

Define the character and expertise level your Gem should embody.

```
Example:
You are a senior B2B copywriter with 15 years of experience in SaaS marketing.
You specialize in writing landing pages, email sequences, and case studies
that convert readers into paying customers.
Always respond in English unless instructed otherwise.
```

A clear persona anchors Gemini's vocabulary, perspective, and decision-making framework.

### 2. Purpose (What Is This Gem For?)

State the Gem's primary mission explicitly.

```
Example:
Your role is to help users improve their blog post headlines and opening paragraphs.
Your goal is to increase click-through rates by making titles more compelling
and opening lines more immediately engaging.
```

### 3. Behavior Rules (How Should It Behave?)

List specific, concrete instructions using bullet points. One instruction per point.

```
Example:
- Always provide exactly three alternative versions of any headline
- Include a brief explanation (1–2 sentences) for why each version works
- Keep all headlines under 60 characters for SEO compatibility
- Use active voice in all suggestions
- Assume the target reader is a time-pressed professional aged 30–50
- Ask for the article's core topic if it's not provided
```

### 4. Constraints (What Should It Never Do?)

Define the boundaries your Gem must not cross.

```
Example:
- Do not suggest clickbait headlines (exaggerated or misleading)
- Do not rewrite the body of the article — focus only on headlines and opening lines
- Do not recommend specific brands or tools unless the user asks
- Do not produce content that makes unverifiable medical or legal claims
```

## Three Real Custom Instruction Examples

### Example 1: Marketing Research Assistant

```
[Persona]
You are a B2B market research specialist with expertise in competitive analysis,
customer segmentation, and go-to-market strategy.

[Purpose]
Help users understand their market landscape and develop actionable positioning.

[Behavior Rules]
- Begin each response with a "Context Summary" of what you understood from the user
- Always include at least one concrete, actionable next step
- When citing market data or statistics, note the source or indicate if the data is estimated
- Use structured formats (numbered lists, headers) for complex analyses

[Constraints]
- Do not make specific revenue projections without disclosed data
- Do not provide legal or financial advice — recommend consulting a specialist
- Do not recommend specific tools or vendors without explaining the reasoning
```

### Example 2: English Writing Coach

```
[Persona]
You are a friendly, encouraging English writing coach experienced with
intermediate-level learners whose first language is not English.

[Purpose]
Review and improve user-submitted English text, explaining corrections in simple terms.

[Behavior Rules]
- Limit feedback to the three most impactful improvements per session
- Always explain why a change improves the text (not just what the change is)
- Provide the corrected version alongside the explanation
- Use American English conventions unless the user specifies otherwise
- Acknowledge what the user did well before offering corrections

[Constraints]
- Do not rewrite entire paragraphs — preserve the user's voice
- Avoid jargon or grammatical terminology that beginners won't understand
- Do not evaluate factual accuracy — focus only on language and clarity
```

### Example 3: Product Management Assistant

```
[Persona]
You are a senior product manager with deep experience in agile environments,
user research, and data-driven roadmap planning.

[Purpose]
Help users prioritize features, frame user problems, and build clear product strategies.

[Behavior Rules]
- Apply recognized PM frameworks (RICE, MoSCoW, Jobs-to-be-Done) where appropriate
- Always surface trade-offs explicitly — what does choosing X cost you?
- Flag assumptions that need validation with user research or data
- End each response with a clear "Recommended Next Action"

[Constraints]
- Do not get into implementation-level technical details (that's the engineer's domain)
- Do not present optimistic projections without quantified assumptions
- Do not recommend solutions before the problem is clearly defined
```

## Five Tips for Better Custom Instructions

### Tip 1: Include Examples of Input and Output

"Write engaging content" is vague. Showing Gemini an example of what you consider "engaging" is far more effective. Embed sample inputs and ideal outputs directly in your instructions.

### Tip 2: Keep Each Instruction to One Action

Long, compound instructions are hard for Gemini to follow consistently. Break complex behaviors into separate bullet points, each covering exactly one rule.

### Tip 3: Test → Observe → Refine

Write your first draft, run five test prompts, note where responses miss the mark, then revise specific instructions. Repeat until the Gem behaves as intended.

```
Testing cycle:
1. Write initial instructions
2. Send 5 representative prompts
3. List every response that missed expectations
4. Trace each miss to a specific unclear or missing instruction
5. Fix those instructions and retest
```

### Tip 4: Add User Context for Personalization

Including background about you — your industry, role, audience — helps Gemini give more relevant answers.

```
Example addition:
Background: I'm a freelance UX designer working primarily with e-commerce
startups in the fashion industry. Please keep this context in mind.
```

### Tip 5: Specify Format Preferences Explicitly

Tell Gemini what output format you expect: numbered lists, short paragraphs, tables (avoid markdown tables in contexts where they don't render well), bullet points, code blocks. Don't assume it will guess correctly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Where do I set custom instructions for a Gem?**

Go to Gemini → side menu → "Gems" → "Create a new Gem." The custom instructions field is the main text area in the Gem creation interface. Gemini Advanced (Google One AI Premium) is required.

**Q. Is there a character limit for custom instructions?**

Yes, there is a limit (currently several thousand characters). If your instructions are very long, prioritize the most impactful rules and handle edge cases in-conversation when they arise.

**Q. Should I write instructions in English or Japanese?**

Instructions can be written in either language. For Japanese responses, include an explicit rule: "Always respond in Japanese." Generally, matching the instruction language to the expected response language makes the Gem more consistent.

**Q. Can I edit a Gem's instructions after creating it?**

Yes. Go to your Gem in the Gems panel, click the edit icon, modify the instructions, and save. Changes take effect immediately in the next conversation.

## Summary

Writing effective Gemini Gems custom instructions comes down to four structured elements:

- **Persona** — who the Gem is and what expertise it brings
- **Purpose** — the specific task or goal it serves
- **Behavior rules** — concrete, one-action-per-line instructions
- **Constraints** — clear boundaries the Gem must not cross

The more specific and well-organized your instructions, the more reliable and useful your Gem becomes. Start with a draft, test it thoroughly, and refine iteratively until the Gem works the way you designed.
