I keep client drafts in a Google Doc on Drive, passing revisions back and forth. The other day I folded in a round of edits, rewrote a whole paragraph, and then asked Gemini to summarize the current draft in three lines.

One of those three lines contained the sentence I had just rewritten away.

I reopened the document. The edit was saved. Sharing hadn't changed. Gemini still handed me the old wording. My first assumption was lag, so I waited a few minutes and asked again, twice. That got me nowhere.

It wasn't lag. **The file wasn't stale — the conversation was.**

## A source stays attached for the whole conversation

There's a short line in Gemini's help about how sources behave. When you add a source, it **remains active for your entire conversation**, giving Gemini consistent context for its responses.

Consistency is usually a gift. When you're asking ten questions about the same document, you don't want to re-attach it every time.

But "remains active" also means that **the content as it stood when you attached it settles into the conversation and stays there**. I had been thinking of a source as a pointer to a file. It behaves less like a pointer and more like context the conversation is already holding.

So when you edit the document and ask again in the same thread, the pre-edit content is still sitting there as raw material. The file moved forward; the conversation didn't. That gap was my three lines.

For reference, here's where the attach controls live. In Docs or Slides, open **Ask Gemini** in the top right, click **Add sources** at the bottom of the panel, then **Add from Drive**. From Drive itself, click **Ask Gemini** in the top right and add under **Your sources** in the left panel. Either way, what you add is bound to the open conversation.

## You can't detach a source mid-thread

The help continues. Because Gemini **can reference sources from a previous turn**, the guidance for excluding a source you already included is to **start a new conversation** and pick what you want in your source settings.

In other words, there is no "remove this from the thread" operation. You can untick a box, but content handed over in an earlier turn can still be referenced.

That stopped me for a moment the first time I read it. The interface shows a source list you can add to and delete from, so it looks like a live roster. What you're actually looking at is a list of **what to prioritize next**, not a way to retract **what already entered the conversation**.

Consistency and freshness rarely come together. Pin a source to the thread and you stop re-attaching it, but the thread stops tracking your edits. Re-read the file on every turn and you stay current, but the ground shifts under a discussion in progress. Neither choice is wrong, and Gemini has picked the first one — once I read it that way, I adjusted my own routine instead of arguing with the product.

**When I ask about a document I just edited, I start the conversation over.** Deciding that one thing removed all the guessing.

It also helps to know what a fresh conversation turns on by default.

Drive, Gmail, Chat, and Calendar are all live unless you say otherwise. Web search is the one that's off. If you don't know that and you ask Gemini to "use only this document," a neighboring email thread can quietly join the answer. Open your source settings once, before the first question, and narrow the locations down.

## "It read everything" is an assumption, not a guarantee

If a fresh conversation still gives you an answer that doesn't line up, there's a second thing to check: how much actually got read.

The help states that each request has a limited processing capacity, and that if you have too many sources or include sources with too much text, **Gemini might base its response on some, but not all, of the content**.

This failure is silent. No error, no warning. I once handed over a long meeting record and asked for the decisions made in the back half, and got a summary of the front half. At the time I assumed my instructions were sloppy. Looking back, the more likely story is that the back half never arrived.

The remedies are unglamorous.

- Send less. Detach the sources that aren't relevant and rebuild the conversation
- Split the file. Break a long document into sections and hand over only the part you're asking about
- Scope a spreadsheet by range. Highlight the cells first, then ask — and note that ranges only cover the sheet tab you're currently on

All three are the same move phrased three ways. **More context does not reliably mean a better answer.** I believed the opposite for a long time, adding every file that looked adjacent and expecting the answer to firm up.

## Open the "Sources" list every time

Under each response, Gemini lists the files that contributed. Making a habit of opening that list is what turned "this answer is wrong somehow" into "I can see roughly why."

The help attaches an honest caveat to it. Gemini might miss a source it actually used, it might cite a document it didn't draw on, and — this is the heavy one — it **can make up a source and present it as factual even when it wasn't part of the request**.

So the list is evidence to check against, not the answer itself.

My compromise is to verify selectively. When a summary is going to become text I send to a client, or the basis for a decision, I open the original document and read the passage with my own eyes. Verifying everything costs more time than I have, so I decided in advance which situations are worth it.

## Reading the symptom

Here's the triage table I actually use.

<tr><th>Symptom</th><th>First suspect</th><th>What to do</th></tr>
<tr><td>An edited-out sentence keeps appearing</td><td>Stale source held by the conversation</td><td>Start a new conversation and re-select sources</td></tr>
<tr><td>Unrelated material bleeds into the answer</td><td>Drive and Gmail on by default</td><td>Narrow search locations in source settings</td></tr>
<tr><td>The back half of a document is missing</td><td>Per-request capacity limit</td><td>Detach sources and split the file</td></tr>
<tr><td>"You need access to some files"</td><td>View permission on the file</td><td>Get view access, then add it again</td></tr>
<tr><td>A PDF can't be selected as a source</td><td>The PDF isn't on Drive yet</td><td>Upload it to Drive first</td></tr>
</table>

The file types you can attach as sources are Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and PDFs — and a PDF has to live on Drive before you can pick it. You also need view permission on anything shared with you, which is worth remembering before you spend twenty minutes hunting for a subtler cause.

## Take the detour once

If you try one thing from all of this, make it this one.

Don't continue the thread you have open. **Start a new conversation, re-attach the document you edited, and ask the identical question again.** If the answer changes, the conversation was the problem. If it doesn't, you've narrowed things down to capacity or permissions, which is real progress.

It feels like a detour the first few times. Measured against the minutes I used to lose squinting at an answer after every round of edits, it has been much cheaper.

I spent a while convinced it was just slow to sync. If this saves someone else that particular stretch of waiting, I'll be glad.
