## Setting Up and Using Aqua Voice: A Step-by-Step Guide

### What is Aqua Voice?

Aqua Voice is a dictation app that drops what you say straight into whatever field your cursor is in. Transcription runs on the company's own Avalon model.

It is not a browser editor you record into and then copy from. The text lands directly in the field you are already typing in, so a note draft and a Gemini prompt box work exactly the same way. That single property — never having to move between apps — is what makes the workflow below practical.

### Initial Setup

1. **Install Aqua Voice**
   - Download it from the [official site](https://aquavoice.com)
   - Available for Mac, Windows, and iPhone

2. **Microphone Permissions**
   - Grant microphone access on first launch
   - Ensure a quiet recording environment

3. **Preferences**
   - Set your speaking language to Japanese
   - Add proper nouns and technical terms to the Custom Dictionary (the Pro plan raises the entry limit)

The free Starter plan gives you 1,000 words to try. Pro is $8/month billed annually.

Note that transcription is processed in the cloud. Keep that in mind when dictating material that needs careful handling.

### The Basic Recording Process

1. Place your cursor in the field where the text should go
2. Hold the hotkey and speak
3. Release, and the text appears in place
4. **Speak freely without worrying about structure** (just express your ideas naturally)
5. Review and correct any transcription errors

**Key Principle**: At this stage, **prioritize capturing ideas over perfection**. Editing comes later.

## The Aqua Voice → Gemini Workflow Architecture

### Three-Step Process Overview

```
[Step 1] Aqua Voice Recording (10–30 minutes)
     ↓
[Step 2] Gemini Advanced Structure, Research & Refinement (20–40 minutes)
     ↓
[Step 3] note Publishing & Optimization (10–15 minutes)
```

### Step 1: Free-Form Ideation via Aqua Voice

After selecting your article topic, use Aqua Voice to record your raw thoughts. It helps to keep this mental framework in mind:

- **Introduction**: Why you're addressing this topic
- **Background**: Current trends and problems
- **How-To**: Concrete techniques and methods
- **Case Studies**: Real success and failure examples
- **Call to Action**: What you want readers to do next

**Voice Recording Tips**:
- Speak clear periods (句点/。) so the system creates line breaks
- Say "・" when listing items
- Use conversational language; avoid overly complex expressions

### Step 2: Gemini Advanced Structure, Research & Refinement

Copy your Aqua Voice text and send this prompt to Gemini Advanced:

```
Please optimize the following voice transcription into a note article:

【Requirements】
- Structure with h2/h3 headings
- 2,000–3,000 characters of body content
- Naturally incorporate SEO keywords
- Explain technical terms on first mention
- Provide concrete examples and actionable techniques
- Include a clear CTA (call-to-action) in the final section

【Input Text】
{Your Aqua Voice transcription}

【Output Format】
Markdown format with this structure:
# Article Title
## Setup and context
## Section 1
### Subsection
...
## Conclusion and CTA
```

Gemini Advanced then automatically:

- **Organizes your thoughts** into a logical structure
- **Fills content gaps** with supplementary information
- **Refines language** from conversational to polished
- **Optimizes for SEO** with keyword placement suggestions

### Step 3: Publishing & Optimization on note

Paste your Gemini-refined article into note and make these adjustments:

- **Title**: Include SEO keywords, keep under 80 characters
- **Body**: Insert 2–3 images (one per major section)
- **Hashtags**: 5–8 relevant tags for discoverability
- **Magazine Assignment**: Add to 2–3 relevant magazines

## Conclusion: Your Next Steps

The Aqua Voice × Gemini workflow is a way to **get an idea out of your head by speaking it, hand the structure to AI, and spend your own attention on judgement alone**.

I use this "say all of it out loud first" approach myself when writing operations notes for my wallpaper apps or copy for my sites. Working as an indie developer, I've found that the time cost isn't the writing — it's the run-up before the writing starts. Dictating removes the run-up.

Keeping to this process tends to produce a few changes:

- Drafting takes less time, which leaves more of it for revision and for replying to readers
- The psychological resistance to starting a piece drops
- On days when you can't write, you can still talk

That said, how income grows depends on your subject and your audience. A tighter process raises your output, but output doesn't convert directly into money. What matters more than volume, I suspect, is whether the same reader comes back a second time.

The key is **consistency over perfection**. Record one piece today, shape it with Gemini, and publish it on note.
