## Mr. Yagi of Uozu Tax Office — A Public Servant Who Fears Being Recorded

In April 2026, I called the Uozu Tax Office in Toyama Prefecture, Japan. My purpose was simple: to explain a five-year history of unjust taxation and seek resolution from the person handling my case.

The official who answered was Mr. Yagi. He barely introduced himself — a fundamental breach of protocol for any public servant taking calls in an official capacity. What followed was far worse.

I walked Mr. Yagi through the entire history: the ¥5 million overcollection of consumption tax on export income, the refusal to process my refund, the convenient expiration of my claim under the statute of limitations, and the retaliatory demand for additional tax payments. I explained that I earn approximately ¥45,000 per month (about $300) while being forced to pay over ¥60,000 per month in tax repayments. I described the five years of adjustment disorder, depression, and insomnia. I told him about the seventeen international art awards and the career I had to suspend.

An official who cannot speak when being recorded is an official whose conduct cannot withstand scrutiny. That alone tells you everything about the state of Japan's tax administration.

## Five Years of Unjust Taxation — What Happened

I am a sole proprietor earning income through AdMob advertising on my iPhone applications. These payments come from Google Ireland — making this an **export transaction** that is legally **exempt from Japan's consumption tax**.

Despite this, I paid approximately **¥5 million (roughly $33,000)** in consumption tax through 2020 due to inadequate guidance from my tax accountant and my own lack of specialized tax knowledge at the time.

In 2021, I filed an amended return with the Miyazaki Tax Office requesting a refund. The office **refused** without legitimate grounds. Instead, they reclassified my future filings as tax-exempt — but crucially, they never returned the overpaid amount.

By 2024, when a tax audit was conducted, the officials informed me that my right to a refund had **expired under the statute of limitations**. The tax office had effectively delayed the refund until it became legally impossible to claim, then reversed course and demanded **additional payments** for the period they themselves had classified as tax-exempt.

This is a textbook violation of the principle of good faith. The authority that blocked my refund now uses the passage of time — which they caused — as grounds to demand even more money.

Today I earn roughly **$300 per month** and pay over **$400 per month** in tax repayments. I have suffered from **adjustment disorder, depression, and chronic insomnia** for five years. My career as an internationally recognized artist has been **completely suspended**.
