Thanks for stopping by Gemini Lab again this week.

Between April 25 and May 1, the most-read pieces clustered around a single theme: moving Gemini-based work from prototype to production. Vertex AI Agent Engine, citation-grounded RAG, AI Studio-to-Vertex migration — readers wanted hands-on patterns for the next step. Here are the five articles that drew the most attention.

## #1: Vertex AI Agent Engine × Gemini 2.5 Pro — A Production Deployment Guide

Vertex AI Agent Engine has matured noticeably over the last few months as a managed runtime for Gemini-based agents. This article walks through taking an agent that runs locally and deploying it to Agent Engine for real production use, with implementation snippets at each step.

The most useful exercise while writing this was drawing the line between "what the agent owns" and "what Agent Engine owns" — state management and tool retries shift their responsibilities the moment you move onto the managed surface. The piece codifies that boundary in working code.

[Vertex AI Agent Engine × Gemini 2.5 Pro — A Production Deployment Guide for Managed Agents](/articles/gemini-advanced/vertex-ai-agent-engine-gemini-production-deployment-guide)

## #2: Google AI Pro vs Ultra — Which Should an Indie Developer Pick?

The Pro-vs-Ultra question comes up constantly from solo developers, and the right answer is rarely "whichever is cheaper." After three months of running both side by side, this article distills the decision criteria into something more actionable than feature charts.

The takeaway readers found most useful: Ultra rewards "long, deep, deliberate" work, while Pro shines for "fast back-and-forth" iteration. Even though Gemini 2.5 Pro powers both, the surrounding limits and behavior change the texture of your daily workflow.

[Google AI Pro vs Ultra: Which Should an Indie Developer Pick? 3 Months of Side-by-Side Use](/articles/gemini-basics/google-ai-pro-vs-ultra-which-to-choose-individual-developer-2026)

## #3: Citation-Grounded RAG with Gemini — Production Patterns for Source Attribution

Anyone shipping RAG in production eventually hits the same wall: the quotes "look right" but turn out to be hallucinated. This article presents a production-ready design that forces Gemini to ground every citation in source text, plus an automated verifier that scores citation quality and triggers a fallback when quality drops.

The most rewarding part to write was the score-and-regenerate fallback — letting a low-quality citation kick the response back to a different model rather than silently shipping a fabrication. If "we have citations but I'm not sure how much I trust them" sounds familiar, this is the playbook.

[Citation-Grounded RAG with Gemini: Production Patterns for Source Attribution and Hallucination Detection](/articles/gemini-api/gemini-rag-citation-source-attribution-production-guide)

## #4: Migrating From AI Studio to Vertex AI — A Solo Developer's Hands-On Walkthrough

This is the field log of moving a working AI Studio prototype onto Vertex AI for real production use. Rather than a cleaned-up reference, it documents where the work actually slowed down — service-account permissions, switching from API keys to Application Default Credentials, and the small bookkeeping that adds up.

If you've shipped a proof of concept in AI Studio and are now wondering "how much of this should I rebuild before going live?", this should give you a realistic sense of the effort involved.

[Migrating Working Code from AI Studio to Vertex AI: A Solo Developer's Hands-On Walkthrough](/articles/gemini-api/ai-studio-to-vertex-ai-code-migration-walkthrough)

## #5: Teaching Gemini Your Own Writing Voice — Prompt Design for Solo Creators

Creators frequently ask whether they can get Gemini to imitate their own writing style. This article combines few-shot examples with system instructions to reproduce a personal voice, while addressing the harder problem of avoiding overfitting — where the model can only produce a flat copy of you instead of writing in your style on new topics.

Voice depends on subtle elements: paragraph rhythm, the cadence of closing sentences, the balance of kanji and kana in Japanese, the "where to break" decisions in English. Picking the right examples for those is half the work, and this article is the practical playbook I wish I had when I started.

[Teaching Gemini Your Own Writing Voice: Prompt Design for Solo Creators, and How to Avoid Overfit](/articles/gemini-advanced/gemini-personal-writing-style-imitation-prompt-design)
