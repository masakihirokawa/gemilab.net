## #3 — Classifying 8,000 App Reviews Overnight With Gemini Batch API

[Implementation notes for classifying 8,000 app reviews overnight with Gemini Batch API — vs. the standard API](/articles/gemini-api/gemini-batch-api-app-reviews-overnight-classification)

A pipeline that tags 8,000 App Store and Google Play reviews along three axes (sentiment, category, priority). Moving it to Batch API cut costs roughly in half and finished overnight (about six hours). The article includes the job-submission code, the error-handling on result retrieval, and the retry loop for failed IDs.

The lesson worth taking away is the hybrid pattern: don't try to keep everything in Batch. Extract failed jobs at the result-fetch stage and retry them on the synchronous API. This made overnight recovery dramatically less painful.

## FAQ

**Q. How do you split work between Gemini and Claude?**
A. I lean on Gemini 2.5 Pro for "scan a lot of documents/code and tell me what you see" work, and Claude for precision-critical structured extraction and code generation. The #1 and #4 articles in this week's picks are textbook examples of that split.

**Q. Does Batch API always halve costs?**
A. Per-request pricing halves, but job submission and result retrieval add overhead, so a small batch can be net-negative. In my experience the breakeven is somewhere around 100 items.
