Reading it before experimenting helps you skip a few cycles of "let me just try it and see."

Read more in [The Complete Project Mariner Guide](/articles/gemini-advanced/google-project-mariner-complete-guide).

## #2: Gemini 2.5 Pro Million-Token Strategy — Treating Long Context as a Design Tool

This article reframes Gemini 2.5 Pro's million-token context window from "you can paste a lot in" into a deliberate design strategy. Specs, archived logs, full source trees — there are real cases where Gemini's reach is the differentiator, and the piece walks through five of them with notes on what to feed in and in what order.

The honest thread running through it is that more context isn't automatically better accuracy. The article shows techniques for keeping context wide while explicitly directing attention to the parts that matter, with reproducible examples. If you've felt that long prompts dilute Gemini's responses, this is worth a careful read.

Find the full breakdown in [Gemini 2.5 Pro Million-Token Strategy](/articles/gemini-advanced/gemini-25-pro-million-token-strategy).

## #3: Gemini Gems — The Complete 2026 Guide to Building Your Own Assistant

A practical guide to using Gemini Gems to build the kind of focused assistant that actually fits your workflow. Gems look simple, but the wording of custom instructions has a big effect on behavior, so having a few reproducible patterns to draw from saves time.

The article covers three common shapes — a writing helper, a code reviewer, and a research-prep companion — with template-grade instruction structures for each. The recommended way to read it is to build one Gem alongside the article. The shift from "convenient lookup" to "trusted partner" tends to happen quickly once you see your own Gem in action.

Implementation details in [Gemini Gems — The Complete 2026 Guide](/articles/gemini-basics/gemini-gems-custom-instructions-complete-guide-2026).

A diagnostic article for the moment when Deep Research returns something that feels surprisingly thin — same handful of sources, repetitive framing, no real depth. Deep Research is strong, but its output is sensitive to the shape of the input, so having a quick troubleshooting flow is valuable.

The most-shared sections are about query granularity and the prompt patterns that nudge Deep Research mid-flight to broaden a narrow source pool. If you write articles backed by Deep Research, this is worth reviewing before each major piece.
