After the Golden Week holidays, I opened Google Play Console and the first page of unanswered 1-star reviews for "Beautiful 4K/HDR Wallpapers" — one of my apps that has crossed 50 million downloads in total — was packed with Japanese, Traditional Chinese, Thai, Russian, Persian, Ukrainian, and Polish. "Too many ads." "Why are dogs and cats in the animal category?" "If you claim 4K, deliver 4K."

I am Masaki Hirokawa, an indie developer since 2014. Review backlogs always pile up right after a major release, so on May 10 and again on May 27 I cleared 71 reviews (41 + 30) across two sessions. I refuse to lean on Google's auto-translate; every reply is hand-written and tilted toward the reviewer's native language. Putting Gemini in the loop for draft translation and vocabulary alignment — but keeping the warm opener and the concrete improvement commitment in human hands — is how I can clear 30–40 reviews in a session without sounding like a bot. This article documents that workflow.

If you're an indie dev who keeps deferring review replies, or you've been pasting auto-translations and watching them flatten the conversation, this is for you.

## Why Gemini only gets "draft translation and vocabulary alignment"

Here's the reasoning up front. Reviews on Google Play, especially the top-ranked ones, sit at the storefront for weeks and shape whether new users tap install. They need to read as a real human reply — not the kind of text that AI-detector instincts pattern-match.

If I let Gemini write end-to-end, I run into three predictable failures:

- **Temperature flattening** — every language ends up with the same template-grade gratitude, and reviewers feel like they're talking to a help-desk bot
- **Vocabulary drift** — "option to hide ads" in one reply becomes "ad removal feature" in the next, which erodes trust across a stream of replies
- **Overuse of formulaic closers** — "We sincerely thank you" and "We appreciate your continued support" start pounding through every language

When I confine Gemini's role to drafting translations, surfacing tone candidates, and holding the line on shared vocabulary, the strengths show up. The split looks like this:

- **Always written by the human**: the warm one-liner at the top ("Thanks a ton!" / "Grazie davvero!" / "本当にありがとうございます"), a concrete improvement commitment ("we lowered ad frequency last week"), and any one-to-two-sentence factual response to hostile reviews
- **Safe for Gemini**: short-language phrasing in tongues where my vocabulary is thin (Thai, Persian, Polish), multilingual variants of a phrase I've already locked in ("option to hide ads" / "광고 비표시 옵션" / "opcję ukrycia reklam"), and candidate closing sentences

With that split, each reply averages 90 seconds to 2 minutes. A 30–40 reply session runs about an hour. Compared with looking up vocabulary in dictionaries one phrase at a time, this is roughly half the wall-clock time.

## Hard rules to avoid Google Play penalties

Before the volume work starts, a few operational rules. These don't matter to Gemini, but they matter to you.

- Cap each session at **30–40 replies**. Anything more should spread to the next day
- If you add more on the same day, **space them by tens of minutes to a few hours**
- Don't paste raw auto-translate output — Google Play hosts the same translation engines and the pattern is recognizable
- No affiliate links, no cross-promo, no social handles in replies (policy violation)

Treat raw Gemini output as belonging to the "auto-translate paste" category. Always rewrite at least one sentence by hand before sending.

## Per-language operating notes

Treat this as either a Gemini system context or a printable cheat sheet.

- **Japanese** — Lead with "本当に嬉しいです！" or "本当にありがとうございます". Avoid "誠にありがとうございます". Drop the subject pronoun, keep the register flat
- **English** — Lean casual: "Thanks a ton!", "Cheers!", "Thanks for the heads up!". Skip emoji by default
- **Traditional Chinese** — "非常感謝您的評價與支持！" / "感謝您的評價！". Most users are in Taiwan; do not use simplified glyphs. If the body is simplified but the device language is Traditional, reply in Traditional
- **Simplified Chinese** — Not yet covered; default to Traditional if device language is ambiguous
- **Italian** — "Grazie davvero per le belle parole!" / "Grazie infinite!" with a warm closing
- **Russian** — "Спасибо большое за отзыв!" Bodies can be in English even with Russian device language; weight the body language but acknowledge the locale
- **Korean** — "이용해 주셔서 정말 감사합니다" / vocabulary: "광고 비표시 옵션" for "option to hide ads"
- **Persian** — "از بازخوردتان متشکریم"; mind right-to-left text direction
- **Ukrainian** — "Дякую за відгук"; never conflate with Russian
- **Thai** — "ขอบคุณมากครับ!"; stick to the male form ครับ across replies
- **Polish** — "Dziękujemy za szczerą opinię" / vocabulary: "opcję ukrycia reklam"

The rule of thumb: the body language of the review wins over the device language. If a review is written in English but the device locale is Polish, reply in Polish — the temperature lands warmer.

## A minimal Gemini prompt

Whether you use the Gemini API or the web app, settle on one prompt shape and stop tinkering. Mine:

```text
You are helping an indie developer draft a Google Play review reply.
The output will be edited by a human, so avoid temperature flattening
and the standard pile of formal greetings.

[Hard rules]
- Reply in the language of the review body
- Use the fixed vocabulary below for the "option to hide ads" phrase:
  Japanese: 広告の非表示オプション
  English: option to hide ads / ad-hide option
  Korean: 광고 비표시 옵션
  Polish: opcję ukrycia reklam
- Never write "free and fully removes ads" or "zero ads"
  (AdMob policy)
- Open with a warm one-liner; avoid generic business greetings
  ("we sincerely thank you" / "we appreciate your continued support")

[Review body]
(paste here)

[Reply structure]
1. A one-line warm thank-you with temperature
2. A factual action statement (we added X in this update / we lowered
   ad frequency last week)
3. A forward-looking improvement note when there's concrete plan

[Output]
Two drafts with slightly different nuance.
Both drafts must use the locked vocabulary identically.
```

The trick is "two drafts." A single draft tempts you to paste as-is. Two drafts force you to ask "which one carries more temperature?" and inject a human-judgment step that breaks the template feel.

Concrete example from May 10. The reviewer was a Thai male user; the body flagged inaccurate category classification. The two drafts Gemini returned:

```text
Draft A:
ขอบคุณมากครับ! สำหรับฟีดแบ็กที่ละเอียดมาก ปัญหาการจัดหมวดหมู่
ที่ท่านระบุเป็นเรื่องที่เราเองก็เห็นด้วยว่ายังต้องปรับปรุง
จะค่อย ๆ ปรับปรุงให้ดีขึ้นนะครับ

Draft B:
ขอบคุณมากครับ! ที่แชร์ฟีดแบ็กให้ครับ การจัดหมวดหมู่ของสัตว์
รวมถึงสุนัขและแมวที่ปะปนกันอยู่ตอนนี้
เราตั้งใจจะปรับปรุงในอัปเดตถัดไปครับ
```

A says "we'll gradually improve" in general terms. B specifically calls out the dog-and-cat mix in the animal category, which is exactly what the reviewer raised. I picked B, added one more "ครับ" for natural cadence, and sent. About 30 seconds of judgment time.

## A dedicated structure for ad complaints

Roughly 40% of reviews are ad-related. With AdMob in the loop, this is unavoidable — and replying poorly to them tanks your rating. My standard structure:

```text
[gratitude + empathy] → [we added the "option to hide ads" in this update]
→ [you can find it in the app's settings screen]
→ [we are continuing to refine the ad behavior]
```

In Japanese:

```text
ご報告ありがとうございます。広告の多さでご不便をおかけして申し訳ございません。
このたびのアップデートで「広告の非表示オプション」を追加しました。
アプリ内の設定画面からご確認いただけます。
表示の挙動も引き続き調整してまいります。
```

In English:

```text
Sorry the ads were too much. We just added an option to hide ads
in the latest update — you can find it in the app settings.
Thanks for the heads up!
```

Translating this template into Russian / Persian / Thai through Gemini is reliable — the output is stable. Watch out for "zero ads" or "completely free of ads" leaking back in. AdMob policy compliance requires that wording to stay neutral; writing "zero ads" today is a future support ticket.

## Don't use Gemini for hostile or coordinated reviews

The hardest replies are hostile reviews and what look like coordinated 1-star posts. Gemini's default reaction is to write something safely neutral, which is fine, but the volume of judgment needed in these cases makes "use Gemini" the wrong default. I write these by hand, short, fact-only.

```text
We're sorry you've had a frustrating experience. If you could let us know
more about what's happening, we'd love to look into it.
You can reach us through the in-app contact form.
```

Coordinated patterns (multiple accounts in the same time window using the same wording) get a polite reply *and* an entry in my watch list. From my own playbook:

- Multiple 1-star reviews from different accounts using the same phrase ("you're terrible," "battery drains")
- Pinpoint-specific identical complaints ("dogs and cats in the animal category," "battery drain")
- Accounts with a "History (2)" badge or higher (multiple posts)
- A long-form complaint that has been pinned at the top of the storefront for weeks

These get monitored, not automated. If a pattern persists, I escalate to Google Play Support. Gemini does not belong in this part of the workflow.

## Reply priority and country focus

When time is scarce:

- **Top public-storefront reviews** — what new installs see first. Use `showAllReviews=true` to find them on the storefront side
- **Unanswered 1-stars** — for rating recovery and churn protection
- **Unanswered 5-stars** — in their native language; helps community building
- **Unanswered 2–4 stars** — the middle ground

Country priority order is **Japan → Taiwan → Italy → Thailand → South Korea → Brazil → United States**. Taiwan and Thailand have particularly large user bases, so Traditional Chinese and Thai must be in native, never auto-translated. Thai with consistent male-form ครับ, Traditional weighted by device language — small details that change the temperature.

When the review body is in a language I don't read (Persian, Ukrainian, Polish for me), I run a quick Gemini pass for an English understanding first, then write the reply back in the original language. Two browser tabs: one running Gemini on the body, one running the reply prompt above. The reply still goes in the user's language, not English.

## Hold yourself to the improvement commitments you made

If your reply promises a concrete improvement, file it in `progress-and-todo-ja.md` or `remaining-tasks-ja.md` immediately. From the 71 replies in May, my future-update queue gained:

- A **search and filter feature** (to relieve thousands-of-pages scrolling fatigue)
- **Position save and resume inside a category**
- **Separating dogs and cats out of the generic animal category**
- **Replacing legacy low-resolution images**
- **De-duplicating repeated images**
- **Ad frequency and close-button visibility tuning**

After committing to an improvement in a reply, I ask Gemini to "turn this thread into a task entry" and paste the result into `remaining-tasks-ja.md`. Gemini is good at extracting action items from prose while preserving nuance. The discipline of matching what you said in replies to what you actually ship next quarter is non-negotiable — that one extra step closes the loop.

A mentor I met online when I was 17 once told me that "art is a natural language open to everyone." I keep that in mind for app replies too — they're part of the product. Gemini helps with the rough sketch; the final brushstroke is human. That distance feels right.

## Checklist to bake into your operations

Final summary, if you're starting fresh:

- Cap sessions at 30–40 replies; spread the work across days
- Pre-stock 2–3 warm opener phrases per language so Gemini doesn't have to regenerate them
- Maintain a single vocabulary table for fixed phrases (like "option to hide ads") across all 11 languages, and pass it into Gemini's context every time
- Always request "two drafts with slightly different nuance"
- Don't run Gemini on hostile or coordinated-spam reviews; write those by hand, short, factual
- Track every improvement commitment in `remaining-tasks-ja.md`; Gemini can help do the extraction
- Always rewrite at least one sentence of Gemini's draft to avoid auto-translate paste detection

Run with this and review replies stop feeling like a chore and start feeling like an hour of tuning the storefront. Thanks for reading.
