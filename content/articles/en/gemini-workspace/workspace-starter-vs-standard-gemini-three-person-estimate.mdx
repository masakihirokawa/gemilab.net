I opened the pricing page in Japanese and in English side by side, and the first thing I noticed was that the English capture showed no dollar amounts at all. The Japanese page had a table, and even the discount dates.

Same company, same product, different information depending on which version you read. That stopped me before I had written a single line of a quote.

Reading the Japanese page top to bottom, I still missed that the discount period can be read two ways. Only when I went back to the footnote did I see that a yearly estimate has to carry a range.

**Before hunting for the cheaper plan, write down what differs and which assumption each number rests on.** Here is how I do that, using a three-person office as the example.

Everything below reflects what Google's pricing page showed on October 30, 2026. Please confirm the discount terms on the checkout screen before you commit.

## First: the plan decides where Gemini shows up

As an indie developer running wallpaper apps, I am not the one buying Workspace seats for staff. Still, "does Gemini work on the cheaper plan?" is a question that keeps coming up whenever you help a small office.

Here are the Gemini-related rows from the comparison table.

Starter does include the Gemini app and Gemini in Gmail, so "Starter has no Gemini" would be wrong. If you want Gemini inside documents or spreadsheets, though, you are in Standard territory. The page itself labels Gemini across the Workspace apps as "limited" on Starter.

## Second: list three tasks and where each one lives

Staring at the plan differences does not settle anything. I write down the office's work first, as three lines at the top of my quote notes:

1. Three tasks done at least weekly (an invoice email, meeting notes, a monthly tally)
2. For each, where you would want Gemini (Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Meet, or the Gemini app)
3. If any task lands in Docs or beyond, Standard is the first candidate; if it is only Gmail and the app, start with Starter

Invoice wording lives in Gmail, so Starter covers it. Turning a Meet call into notes, or repairing a formula inside Sheets, pushes you toward Standard.

The line comes from where the work sits, not from the price. It is the same habit I use when a shared Gem stalls: decide where it is blocked before asking anyone. I wrote that one up in [the three places Gem sharing stops](/articles/gemini-workspace/gem-sharing-three-layers-check-order).

## Third: the discount reads two ways on the same page

The Japanese page lists a monthly price per user in USD, excluding tax. The discount window is 2026/11/10 to 2027/02/10 at 50% off, and the regular price applies afterwards.

<table>
<thead><tr><th>Plan</th><th>During discount</th><th>From 2027/02/10</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Starter</td><td>$3.50</td><td>$7.00</td></tr>
<tr><td>Standard</td><td>$7.00</td><td>$14.00</td></tr>
<tr><td>Plus</td><td>$11.00</td><td>$22.00</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

The wording is the problem. The discount label says "50% off for 3 months," while a footnote says the trial price applies for 12 months to the first 20 users added, and that after 12 months the standard price applies to everyone.

For three seats, I could not tell from the page alone whether the discount ends after 3 months or lasts 12. I would rather not pretend to know, so I price both readings.

```python
# Annual cost (USD, before tax) for 3 seats under each reading of the discount
users = 3
plans = {"Starter": 7.0, "Standard": 14.0, "Plus": 22.0}  # regular monthly price per seat

for name, full in plans.items():
    half = full / 2
    reading_a = users * (3 * half + 9 * full)  # Reading A: discount lasts 3 months only
    reading_b = users * (12 * half)            # Reading B: discount lasts all 12 months
    no_discount = users * 12 * full            # ceiling with no discount
    print(f"{name:9s} A={reading_a:7.1f}  B={reading_b:7.1f}  ceiling={no_discount:7.1f}")
```

Running it gives this.

<tbody>
<tr><td>Starter</td><td>$220.5</td><td>$126.0</td><td>$252.0</td></tr>
<tr><td>Standard</td><td>$441.0</td><td>$252.0</td><td>$504.0</td></tr>
<tr><td>Plus</td><td>$693.0</td><td>$396.0</td><td>$792.0</td></tr>

</table>

The gap between Starter and Standard is $220.5 a year under Reading A, $126.0 under Reading B, and $252.0 with no discount. Per seat, that is between $3.50 and $7.00 a month.

Seeing that range made me reconsider: this is not a decision about price. With the gap this small, choosing by where the work lives (the second point) is less likely to leave you regretting it later.

## Four lines I keep in every quote note

- The date I checked, and which language version of the page I read
- Currency and tax handling (here: USD, before tax; the page showed no yen amount)
- Both annual totals whenever a discount can be read two ways
- For each task, where Gemini needs to be available

The final amount, including conversion and tax, appears at checkout. Not taking a number off the pricing page straight into an internal approval is a line I try to hold.
