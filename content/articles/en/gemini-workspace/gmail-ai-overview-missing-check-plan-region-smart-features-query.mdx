I was rereading the September 15 Google Workspace Updates post when one line stopped me. For personal accounts, Japan is not on the list.

The feature lets you type a plain-language question into Gmail's search bar and get a summary, built from your own email, above the results. As an indie developer who digs through store notifications and review emails, I could see the appeal right away. Then I followed the conditions one by one and found that the people who get it and the people who don't are split quite sharply.

What I'd like to say first is that "just wait" is no longer a good answer. The rollout windows Google gave all closed in September. If you're reading this in November and still see nothing, checking your own side comes before waiting.

Add the days up and even Rapid Release finishes in mid-September and Scheduled Release in late September. More than a month has passed, so "my turn just hasn't come yet" no longer fits what Google published.

The help page does still carry a note that the feature is rolling out gradually and may not be available to you. I wrote separately about how to count a rollout wait yourself: [How to tell a Gemini feature that is still rolling out from one that is broken](/en/articles/gemini-workspace/gemini-workspace-feature-rollout-wait-or-fault-triage). This article covers what to check once waiting is ruled out.

## Check 1 — Account type, plan, and region

Start with whether your account is eligible at all. The help page says:

- Personal accounts: you need Google AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra. Accounts enrolled in Google Workspace Experiments, a trusted-tester program, can also use it
- Work or school accounts: you need an eligible Workspace plan. The announcement listed Business Starter, Standard and Plus, Enterprise Starter, Standard and Plus, and Frontline Plus, among others

The easiest line to miss is the regional restriction for personal accounts. The help page says the feature is unavailable if you live in the European Economic Area, Japan, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom. So a personal Gmail account in Japan will not show it even with a paid Google AI plan. That is neither a billing problem nor a settings problem.

That restriction is written for personal accounts, though. If a work account shows nothing, look at the smart-feature settings and your admin's configuration instead of region.

## Check 2 — Both smart-feature switches

If the account is eligible and nothing appears, look at smart features. The end-user section of the announcement says both of these must be on:

1. Smart features in Gmail, Chat, and Meet
2. Google Workspace smart features

Having one on and the other off is not unusual. When two similarly named options sit in different places, it's easy to lose track of which one you touched. I'd assume there are two and go look at each.

One caution before you switch anything. According to the help page, turning smart features off also turns off other AI-powered Workspace features. The reverse holds too: if you had turned them off for some reason, turning them back on will wake those other features as well.

For work accounts there is an admin-side condition. The announcement says the feature is on by default when both "Gemini for Workspace in Gmail" and "Workspace Intelligence access to Gmail" are enabled. You can't see those as a user, so I've drafted a message further down.

## Check 3 — The display language

The announcement describes this expansion as going to paid-plan users whose Gmail language is set to English, and notes that it was previously limited to English-language users in the US.

The help page, meanwhile, says the feature is available in additional languages and points to a separate page of supported languages. I haven't confirmed whether it works with a Japanese display language, and I'd rather not guess. Please check that page.

As a way to isolate the cause, you can switch Gmail's display language to English for a moment and run the same search. If the overview appears, language was the factor. If not, it's something else. Switch the language back afterward.

## Check 4 — How you write the search

If the account and settings are fine, one last area remains. The help page spells out two points that are easy to overlook:

- Search operators suppress the overview. A search using `is:unread` or `from:` is one example
- It is optimized for natural-language questions. If nothing appears, try a more descriptive phrase, phrased as a question

Plenty of people type `from:` first out of habit, and may have concluded "it never shows up" for that reason alone. Try rewriting the search like this:

```text
(no overview)  from:store-notice is:unread review
(try this)     What did the review result email from last week say?
```

The steps are: open Gmail on a computer, type into the search box, and press Enter. The help page describes the computer version, so if your phone shows nothing, try a computer first.

## What to send your admin

If you've worked through all of that and still see nothing, it's time to ask your admin. Writing down what you've already checked gets you a faster reply.

```text
Subject: Gmail search AI Overviews not showing for my account

AI Overviews in Gmail search don't appear on my account.
I have already checked the following:
- The rollout window (September 3 to late September) has passed
- Both "Smart features in Gmail, Chat, and Meet" and
  "Google Workspace smart features" are on
- I tried a plain question with no search operators

Could you check two things?
1. Is Gemini for Workspace in Gmail enabled?
2. Is Workspace Intelligence access to Gmail enabled?
```

Please only list something as checked if you really checked it. If the message doesn't match the facts, your admin may chase the wrong cause.

**Before asking an admin about a missing feature, rule out your own conditions from the top.** Doing so keeps the request short and makes it easier to accept an honest "not eligible."

For your next step, remove operators like `is:unread` from the search box and type only a one-sentence question. If the overview appears, the search wording was the cause. If not, walk back through the list starting with the account type. That's the order I plan to keep using myself.
